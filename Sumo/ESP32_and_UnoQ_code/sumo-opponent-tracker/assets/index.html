Mask - what the detector sees

green outline = chosen group (gray = not confirmed yet)  |  red dot = its center  |  yellow line = horizon  |  magenta line = own-robot line  |  orange box = the spot that'll be sampled

Pick a single color in the dropdown above the mask if the "combined" view looks too messy to make sense of - that shows you exactly which one color is lighting up too much.

Fast color tuning

Click the spot on the live image above where a color is (the orange box jumps there), then press the matching button. This reads the real pixels and sets a snug range instantly. Clicking the same color somewhere else and pressing its button again widens the range to also cover that spot - so a couple of clicks across light and shadow on a piece works better than one click.

Horizon & own-robot lines

Click where the line should be on the live image above, then press the matching button.

Advanced settings (most setups won't need these - defaults are already reasonable)

Cleanup & rejection thresholds

Only the first three (blur, morph open, morph close) change what you see in the mask picture above. The rest only affect whether a shape counts as "a robot" after the mask - they won't visibly change the mask itself.

Opponent colors (manual fine-tuning)

Usually you won't need these - "Fast color tuning" above sets a good range in one click. Use these only to nudge a range afterward.

Distance calibration

Place a robot (or LEGO stand-in) at a measured distance from the camera, enter that distance below, then click Capture. Repeat at several distances (e.g. 10, 20, 30, 50, 75 cm).

Note: distance_cm is currently computed from NEAR_CM / FAR_CM in python/main.py, not from these points.
